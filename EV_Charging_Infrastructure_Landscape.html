Participants are measured at TWO non-comparable stages because the value chain splits there: (1) the CHARGE-POINT-OPERATOR (CPO) stage for the public-DC channel — CPOs are the end customers who deploy the installed CAPEX that the sizing counts, so they map to the sized-market basis but their public share data exists only as CHARGE-POINT COUNTS, not euro spend; and (2) the HARDWARE MANUFACTURER / wallbox-OEM stage for the residential and workplace/commercial channels — these players sell hardware (manufacturer price) and their share is reported on hardware units or hardware revenue, NOT on the EUR 5.5–8.0bn end-user installed-spend denominator. Players at these two stages CANNOT be ranked against each other (a CPO's network and an OEM's hardware revenue are different bases), and NEITHER set of public shares is denominated in the sized end-user-spend figure. Full route-to-market interpretation

The market splits into a B2C residential retrofit channel, a B2B public-DC channel owned/operated by Charge Point Operators (CPOs), and a B2B workplace/commercial AC channel; their price builds differ sharply (residential and commercial are hardware-resale + electrician install, while public DC is dominated by grid/civil works), so blending them double-counts installation and misstates the hardware-revenue base used for sizing.

Technical notes (7)
  • VAT assumed at 20% across all channels; EU27 standard VAT actually ranges 17% (Luxembourg) to 27% (Hungary). For the two B2B channels (public DC, commercial AC) VAT is typically reclaimable, so the ex-VAT figure is the true economic anchor.
  • Distributor gross margins (15-25%) used in the residential and commercial waterfalls are HYPOTHESES — only EBIT/EBITA margins (5-7% for Rexel/Sonepar) are sourced; product-level gross margin is not publicly disclosed.
  • Installation labour is a service value-add, NOT a pure resale markup. The consumer-to-manufacturer ratios therefore largely reflect installation/civil intensity, not channel margin stacking — sizing on a manufacturer-revenue basis must not treat install spend as hardware.
  • Public-DC waterfall assumes large CPOs buy hardware DIRECT from OEMs (no distributor stage); smaller operators buying via distributors would add ~10-20% and lift the ratio.
  • Per-kWh public charging revenue paid by drivers (CPO service margin) is a SEPARATE charging-as-a-service market and is deliberately EXCLUDED from this infrastructure/CAPEX waterfall to avoid scope creep and double-counting.
  • Anchor prices are representative mid-market units (11kW AC home, 150kW DC public, 22kW AC commercial); site-specific grid upgrades, transformers or trenching can push DC project totals far above the €72k anchor (NREL: electrical infra can exceed hardware cost at complex sites).
  • Unit-level manufacturer price hardware prices rely substantially on a single source (Virta for DC) or are derived from installed-cost splits; treat all manufacturer price figures as RESEARCHED/HYPOTHESIS, not CONFIRMED — no figure here met the ≥2-independent-source bar for the manufacturer price level.

Macro sensitivity model (5)
Copper price
Direct input-cost exposure across all three channels — chargers, cabling, transformers and grid connections are copper-intensive (an EV uses ~80–100 kg Cu; charging hardware and the civil/grid scope add more). LME copper hit record highs of ~USD 12,000–13,387/t around Dec 2025–Jan 2026, up ~41% in 2025. 2026 forecasts diverge: Goldman ~USD 10,710/t H1 (surplus view) vs JPMorgan >USD 12,000/t (deficit view); ICSG flags a 2026 refined deficit. Sustained high copper inflates the hardware-resale base of residential/commercial channels and the grid/civil scope of public DC.
Industrial electricity prices (EU)
Demand-side and CPO-opex exposure. EU industrial power averages ~2× US and ~50× above China levels and stayed elevated through 2025. High electricity raises CPO operating cost and consumer charging tariffs (public AC ~EUR 0.30–0.50/kWh, DC fast ~EUR 0.50–0.80/kWh) — worsening the public-DC utilization/profitability squeeze and weakening the relative TCO case vs. cheaper home charging (~EUR 0.10–0.40/kWh). Pressures the most fragile channel (public DC) hardest; favours residential.
Interest rates / cost of capital
Concentrated on the PUBLIC-DC (CPO) channel, which is debt-financed and CAPEX-heavy (~EUR 72,000 turnkey per 150 kW DC port). Higher financing cost compounds the falling-utilization profitability crisis flagged upstream — a CPO funds the asset against thin throughput, so rate sensitivity is high; residential/commercial (hardware-resale + electrician install, low CAPEX intensity) are far less exposed. Quantification of channel-level rate elasticity not found in public sources — directional only.
Steel price / electricity-in-steel pass-through
Secondary input cost, concentrated in public-DC civil works (foundations, enclosures, canopies) and to a lesser extent commercial AC pedestals. EU steel competitiveness is squeezed by high power — electricity's share of EU steel selling price rose from ~7% (2021) to ~20% (2025) — so the electricity shock partly re-enters this market via steel. Smaller magnitude than copper.
FX (EUR/USD, EUR/CNY)

Net: tailwind-dominant through ~2027 (AFIR + EPBD are locked in and date-driven), turning more contingent 2028–2035 as CO2 dilution and the still-unlegislated Clean Corporate Vehicles Regulation determine whether the workplace and residential channels keep compounding. Public DC is insulated on unit count by the AFIR power floor and replacement churn but not on profitability, where high electricity and rates bite.

Methodology & how to read this

How this study is built, and what the confidence labels mean.

Confidence
CONFIRMED — ≥2 independent sources agree (or independently verified)
RESEARCHED — backed by one cited source
HYPOTHESIS — the model's estimate — unverified, given as a range
CONFLICTING — sources disagree beyond tolerance — shown as a range
SINGLE_SOURCE — only one source found
Audit verdicts
HOLDS — independently corroborated
OVERSTATED — right direction, magnitude too strong
REFUTED — contradicted by what was found
UNCERTAIN — could not be independently confirmed
Source quality
Authoritative — official statistics, regulators, standards bodies, research institutes
Reputable — established press and major industry research
Other — vendor, trade or unattributed — read with care

The pipeline

The market is researched as a fixed sequence of specialist stages, each building on what the earlier ones proved. Every figure is sourced or flagged: a number that cannot be traced is never stated.

1
Lead Plan. the approved boundary, declared evidence needs and confirmation criteria.
2
Data Pack. the mapped deterministic APIs and source-traced series retrieved before agent analysis.
3
Value Chain. the routes to market (channels) and the price build-up from end-user price back to manufacturer price — the one margin.
4
Industry Structure & Value Pools. submarkets, component economics, manufacturers, concentration and control points.
5
Demand. the end-user demand equation, the growth drivers vs barriers, and what would have to be true for each.
6
Market Size. ≥2 independent sizing pathways, reconciled on one declared basis — the answer is a range, not a point.
7
Participants. who plays at each value-chain stage, their share of the sized market, and concentration.
8
Regulatory & Macro. the named regulations (tailwind/headwind), their timelines, and macro sensitivities.
9
Verification. an adversarial audit that tries to refute the decision-critical claims.
10
Evidence Repair. one bounded attempt to narrow, support or retract failed material claims, followed by re-verification.
11
Expert Panel. 20–30 named people across at least five stakeholder groups, with identity, expertise, claim proximity and affiliations independently checked.
12
Quotation Selection. on-point supporting and challenging voices selected only from the verified panel, followed by exact-source reopening.
13
Synthesis. the executive read — written only from eligible claims; panel opinion remains interpretive context.

Key terms

Manufacturer price
The manufacturer's selling price at its own premises — before transport, distributor and retailer margins, and VAT. The smallest, 'truest' measure of a market: manufacturer revenue.
End-user / installed spend
What the final buyer actually pays — manufacturer price plus every downstream margin, plus (for services) installation/labour and VAT. Often 2–10× the manufacturer price figure.
Consumer↔manufacturer ratio
The multiple between end-user price and manufacturer price for a channel — used to move a market size between the two bases without distorting it.
Channel
A distinct route a product takes to its buyer (e.g. OEM first-fit vs aftermarket). Price builds up differently per channel, so they are never blended.
Triangulation
Sizing a market by ≥2 independent methods and reconciling them; agreement raises confidence, divergence is itself a finding.
TAM / SAM / SOM
Total / Serviceable / Obtainable market — the whole market, the slice a player can address, and the slice it can realistically win.
Concentration (CR4 / HHI)
How consolidated a market is — CR4 = combined share of the top 4 players; HHI = sum of squared shares.
