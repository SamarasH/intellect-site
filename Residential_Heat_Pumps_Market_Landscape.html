Residential Heat Pumps Market Landscape — Executive Read

EU27 · written from verified claims only

The €4.3–5.2B EU27 ex-works market (CY2024) is structurally policy-backed but gated by one largely un-regulated lever — the elec/gas price ratio — which drove a ~22% sales collapse in 2024.
Residential heat pumps in EU27 are a ~€4.3–5.2B ex-works market in a 2024 cyclical trough (-22% YoY), with a stacked pro-HP regulatory package building toward 2027–2030+. The decisive demand gate — retail electricity must be ≲2x gas — sits outside that regulatory perimeter and broke in 2024. Manufacturer concentration is moderate and consolidating at the top while Chinese OEMs fragment the value end.

Value Chain — residential heat pumps

EU27 · one chain, 3 route(s) to market

The dominant route is the three-tier hydronic retrofit trade where installation labour (not the unit) is the majority of spend; new-build is more direct and procurement-led, and air-to-air is a separate cheaper retail product. Blending them would double-count installation and badly distort any move between installed-spend and manufacturer-revenue sizing because the consumer/manufacturer ratio differs ~2x across channels (3.5x vs 1.9x vs 2.3x).
Routes to market: Retrofit / aftermarket — hydronic air-to-water (three-tier trade: manufacturer -> heating wholesaler/distributor -> installer/HVAC contractor -> homeowner) New-build / OEM-developer — air-to-water (project/bulk, semi-direct to developer-general contractor, embedded in dwelling price) Air-to-air split — retail / B2C reversible units (manufacturer -> distributor -> retailer/e-commerce -> consumer, lighter install) shared spine

Flagged assumptions & risks

  • geography skew: anchors use Germany, the most expensive EU27 market (avg ~EUR 28-30k retrofit). EU27-average installed prices are roughly half (France EUR 12-20k, NL EUR 11-19k, UK EUR 10-14k), so the absolute waterfalls overstate a pan-EU27 figure; the consumer_to_manufacturer ratio is more transferable than the absolute prices but still varies with local labour share.
  • ratio IS LABOUR-driven: the 3.50x retrofit ratio is high because German installation labour/ancillaries (~55% of net) dwarf the unit cost; in lower-labour EU markets the ratio compresses toward ~2.5x. Use a channel- and country-specific ratio for sizing, never one blended number.
  • MARGIN sources are US-leaning: distributor (15-25%) and installer-markup (20-50%) percentages come predominantly from US/North-American HVAC trade sources; no EU-specific published margin study was found. EU hydronic markups are likely at the lower end. Treat all margin_pct as RESEARCHED-at-best, not CONFIRMED.
  • new-BUILD ANCHOR IS indirect: in the new-build channel the heat pump price is embedded in the dwelling sale and never seen by the end buyer; the anchor is the per-unit installed cost to the developer/contractor, and the final distribution margin is a HYPOTHESIS (10-20%).
  • AIR-TO-AIR ANCHOR UNVERIFIED: the EUR 2,500 single-split anchor and its install split are HYPOTHESES (range EUR 1,500-4,000); no clean cited EU27 retail price was obtained. The whole channel C waterfall is low-confidence.
  • CHANNEL-MIX unknown here: this agent does not weight the channels. Sizing must apply each channel's ratio to that channel's own volume/value share — the EU split between wholesaler-routed vs direct-to-installer hydronic, new-build vs retrofit, and air-to-water vs air-to-air is out of scope and must come from the sizing agent.
  • CHANNEL overlap: many distributors and the energy-utility 'heat-as-a-service'/bundled route (EDF, German/Italian/Spanish energy retailers per Agora) are not modelled as a separate waterfall; they largely re-use the retrofit trade chain plus a financing/service wrapper and could warrant a 4th channel if leasing/HaaS is material.

Regulatory & Macro — residential heat pumps

EU27 · what bounds the trajectory

ETS2 — EU Emissions Trading System for buildings & road transport (Directive (EU) 2023/959, Fit-for-55)tailwind CONFIRMED src
Adopted 2023; carbon price on fuels for building heating starts 2027 (possible 1-year delay to 2028 if energy prices spike); price-stability mechanism caps allowance price ~EUR 45/t (2020 prices) in early years.
Fuel suppliers must surrender allowances for CO2 emitted by fossil fuels (gas, oil) combusted in buildings — a carbon cost passed through to households' heating-fuel bills. → Directly worsens fossil-heating opex vs electricity, attacking the binding 'OpexEconomicGate (elec/gas ratio)' in the retrofit demand equation. Modelling underpinning the package assumes ~50m heat pumps deployed by 2030; buildings emissions to fall ~24% to 287 Mt. Strongest lever on the dominant hydronic-retrofit channel.
Compliance cost: Cost falls on fossil fuel, not on heat pumps — estimated +EUR 0.09–0.20 per Sm3 of gas at EUR 45–100/t CO2 (≈ +10–25% on a typical gas bill at the upper price). No direct compliance cost to HP manufacturers/installers.
EPBD recast — Energy Performance of Buildings Directive (Directive (EU) 2024/1275)tailwind CONFIRMED src
Entered into force 28 May 2024; national transposition deadline 29 May 2026. Ban on financial incentives for stand-alone fossil-fuel boilers from 1 January 2025. Member-State plans to phase out fossil boilers targeting 2040 (indicative).
Art. 17(15): no public subsidies for stand-alone fossil-fuel boilers from 2025. Art. 3/Annex II: National Building Renovation Plans must set out policies for complete fossil-boiler phase-out; end-of-life replacements to default to non-fossil tech (heat pumps). Zero-emission building standards for new builds. → Tailwind for both channels: removes subsidy competition for gas boilers (retrofit) and pushes HP as default in new dwellings (raising HP_ShareOfNewHeatingInstalls). Indirect — relies on Member-State transposition and credible national plans rather than a hard EU device ban.
Compliance cost: No direct device-compliance cost on heat pumps; cost is borne by the fossil-boiler alternative losing subsidy eligibility. Member-State administrative cost for renovation plans.
F-gas Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2024/573)mixed CONFIRMED src
Adopted 7 February 2024, applies from 11 March 2024. HFC quota cut to 42.9 Mt CO2eq for 2025–2026 (−48% vs 2023), halved again to ~21.7 Mt for 2027–2029; full HFC phase-out by 2050. From 2027: self-contained (monoblock) AC and heat pumps ≤12 kW must use refrigerant with GWP < 150.
Steep HFC supply quota reduction plus product-level GWP caps forcing a switch to low-GWP / natural refrigerants (R290 propane GWP≈3, R32 GWP≈675 is above the 150 cap for small monoblocks from 2027). → Mixed. Tailwind long-term (natural-refrigerant HPs are decarbonisation-aligned, R290 exempt from HFC quota) but a near-term supply/cost headwind: HFC scarcity raises refrigerant prices and risks constraining HP output. Regulation contains a review clause that can release extra HP quota (up to ~10.3% extra in 2025-26, ~6.6% in 2027-29) if REPowerEU HP targets are threatened.
Compliance cost: R290 redesign adds explosion-proof electricals, leak sensors, flame arrestors, stricter assembly and installation safety distances — raises BOM and install complexity (qualitative; per-unit figure not reliably sourced). Partly offset by smaller refrigerant charge (≈50–80 g vs 150–200 g) and exemption from HFC quota.
RED III — Renewable Energy Directive (Directive (EU) 2023/2413)tailwind CONFIRMED src
Adopted October 2023; transposition by mid-2025. Heating & cooling renewable share to rise +0.8 pp per year to 2026, then +1.1 pp per year 2026–2030; indicative 49% renewable energy in buildings by 2030.
Binding national increases in renewable heating share; heat pumps (capturing ambient/aerothermal energy) explicitly count and are the primary tool to hit the buildings target. → Tailwind — sets the demand pull at national level for renewable heating, of which HPs are the dominant pathway. Effect is diffuse/indirect (no per-building device mandate) and criticised as 'business as usual' for not forcing efficient-renewable heating specifically.
Compliance cost: No direct cost on HP equipment; Member-State policy/administrative cost to meet annual heating-share increments.
Ecodesign & Energy Labelling for space/water heaters — revision of Reg. (EU) 813/2013 & 811/2013tailwind RESEARCHED src
Original 2013 rules in force; Commission public consultation on revised draft regulations late 2025. Proposed effective dates not yet final — draft would end placing of fossil-fuel-only boilers on the market by 2029 and rescale energy labels from A+++–D to A–G.
Raises minimum seasonal efficiency (proposals discussed up to ~115% to favour HPs), updates test methods/noise/NOx, and (draft) prohibits stand-alone fossil boilers on the market from 2029; clearer A–G label aids HP comparison. → Tailwind — a market-placement ban on fossil-only boilers (if adopted) is a harder constraint than EPBD's subsidy/phase-out plans, directly removing the competing product. Direction is clear; magnitude and date are proposal-stage, not law.
Compliance cost: Re-testing, re-labelling and minimum-efficiency engineering costs for manufacturers (not quantified); fossil-boiler makers bear the larger burden.
Germany Building Energy Act / GEG ('Heizungsgesetz', Gebäudeenergiegesetz 2024) + BEG subsidymixed CONFIRMED src
In force 1 January 2024. 65%-renewable rule applies immediately to new heating in new-build; phased for existing buildings tied to municipal heat-planning deadlines (large cities mid-2026, others mid-2028). BEG 'speed bonus' (+20%) available for switches before 2028 (then tapers).
Every newly installed heating system must run on ≥65% renewable energy — effectively the HP default; existing working fossil systems may continue/be repaired until end-of-life. BEG grants cover 30–70% of HP install cost (30% base, +20% speed bonus, +30% income-based <EUR 40k, capped at 70%, via KfW). → Material because Germany is the value-chain anchor and largest EU market. Mixed in practice: structurally pro-HP, but the controversy, U-turns and budget tightening around GEG/BEG were a primary cause of Germany's ~48% sales crash in 2024 — illustrating how national fiscal/political volatility can override the EU tailwind.
Compliance cost: Net negative cost to the homeowner (subsidy reduces effective HP price by 30–70%); fiscal cost to the State; uncertainty/whipsaw cost to manufacturers via demand volatility.
REPowerEU & EU Heat Pump Action Plan (policy framework, non-binding targets)tailwind RESEARCHED src
REPowerEU plan 2022; targets ~20m installed HPs by 2027 and ~60m by 2030 (water-based, all-types). Heat Pump Action Plan repeatedly delayed.
Political deployment targets and intended supportive measures (skills, permitting, demand signals); not legally binding on devices or Member States. → Tailwind as the umbrella ambition coordinating the binding instruments above, but on current run-rate (≈2.1–2.3m units per year in 2024, down ~21–22%) the EU is widely assessed as on track to MISS the 60m target — so its standalone demand pull is aspirational.
Compliance cost: None (non-binding).
Social Climate Fund (Regulation (EU) 2023/955)tailwind RESEARCHED src
Established 2023; operational from 2026 ahead of ETS2; runs to 2032. ~EUR 87bn earmarked.
Channels ETS2 revenue to vulnerable/energy-poor households and micro-enterprises, including direct support for clean-heating (heat pump) investment and to cushion carbon-cost pass-through. → Tailwind — subsidises HP uptake precisely in the income segments most exposed to ETS2, supporting the retrofit channel's capture rate; estimates suggest it could fund HPs for up to ~20m energy-poor households. Magnitude per-country still to be allocated.
Compliance cost: None on equipment; it is a funding source.

Macro sensitivity

  • Electricity-to-gas price ratio (the 'spark spread' / OpexEconomicGate): The single most material demand lever and the binding gate in the retrofit demand equation. A HP is a sound investment only when retail electricity is ≲2x gas (per kWh); across much of the EU the ratio sits >2.5x, and in Poland ~4:1, making HP running cost unattractive. This ratio — set by wholesale energy markets, levies and taxes, only partly moved by ETS2 — was a primary driver of the 2024 demand collapse and largely sits outside the regulatory perimeter. CONFIRMED
  • Interest rates / cost of capital: High exposure on both channels. HP retrofit is a large lumpy capex (≈EUR 28k installed, single-family DE anchor) typically financed; new-build demand is geared to construction/mortgage activity. High 2024 rates compounded the cost-of-living squeeze and a ~1% EU drop in building permits, suppressing demand; industry attributes part of the expected 2025–2026 ~15–20% recovery to easing financing conditions. Direction: rate cuts = tailwind, hikes = headwind. RESEARCHED
  • Installation labour cost & installer availability: Highest single cost-base exposure on the dominant retrofit channel: labour, not the unit, is the majority of the EUR 28k installed spend (consumer↔manufacturer ratio 3.5x for hydronic retrofit vs 1.9x new-build). HP installer wage inflation and skilled-installer scarcity therefore move installed price more than any commodity; also a throughput constraint on hitting deployment targets. Lighter for air-to-air retail (simpler install). RESEARCHED
  • Copper price: Moderate-to-low exposure on the ex-works cost, but elevated. LME copper hit records ~USD 12,400–14,500/t in late-2025/early-2026. A ~5 kW HP coil uses only ~1.7–3.1 kg of tube copper plus ~1–5 lb in the compressor — a low single-digit % of the ~EUR 5,700–6,700 ex-works unit, and the unit is itself the minority of installed spend. Manufacturers mitigate via small-diameter tube. Margin pressure on OEMs, negligible on installed price. RESEARCHED
  • Steel price: Low direct exposure (casings, frames, hydronic components). A minor share of BOM; not a swing factor on installed price. CBAM (carbon border adjustment on imported steel/aluminium, definitive regime from 2026) is a secondary upstream cost channel but immaterial at the HP unit level. Flagged as low-materiality. HYPOTHESIS
  • FX (EUR/USD, EUR/CNY): Channel-dependent. EU-domiciled OEM production is largely EUR-denominated, but a growing share of low-cost air-to-water and most air-to-air split units are imported from China — exposing landed cost to EUR/CNY and to USD-priced commodities (copper, refrigerant feedstock). A weaker EUR raises import COGS, advantaging EU manufacturers; impact concentrated in the price-sensitive retail air-to-air channel. Not quantified from a primary source. HYPOTHESIS
  • HFC refrigerant price (F-gas-quota-driven input cost): Direct input-cost exposure created by the F-gas quota: the 2025-26 quota (−48%) and 2027-29 halving structurally tighten HFC supply (R32 etc.), pushing refrigerant prices up and incentivising the R290 switch. Charge mass is small, so refrigerant is a low % of BOM, but supply scarcity is also a volume/availability risk for HFC-based lines through the transition. Natural refrigerant (R290) supply is abundant and quota-exempt. RESEARCHED
Net read. The regulatory tilt is decisively net positive on a structural 2025–2030+ horizon: a stacked, mutually-reinforcing package — ETS2 carbon-pricing of heating fuels (2027), EPBD fossil-boiler phase-out plus the 2025 ban on fossil-boiler subsidies, RED III renewable-heating mandates, the proposed 2029 Ecodesign fossil-boiler market ban, and national mandates like Germany's GEG — collectively forces fossil heating out and makes the heat pump the default replacement. but the near-term (2024–2025) reality is a headwind: EU sales fell ~21–22% in 2024, driven not by regulation but by national subsidy whiplash (Germany BEG) and, above all, an unfavourable electricity-to-gas price ratio (>2.5x vs the ~2x needed) — a macro/energy-market variable that sits largely outside this regulatory perimeter. The single most material instrument is ETS2: it is the one regulatory lever that directly attacks the binding constraint (the elec/gas opex gate) by loading a carbon cost onto gas/oil from 2027, and it funds the offsetting Social Climate Fund — though its real bite depends on the allowance price holding above the ~EUR 45/t stabiliser and surviving cost-of-living politics. F-gas is the principal mixed/headwind item, a manageable supply-cost transition rather than a demand brake.
  • DEMAND'S binding constraint IS largely UN-regulated: the electricity-to-gas price ratio (the OpexEconomicGate) is set by energy markets, levies and national taxation; ETS2 nudges it but does not control it. A positive regulatory stack can still coexist with falling demand if electricity stays >2.5x gas — exactly what happened in 2024.
  • national fiscal volatility overrides EU tailwind: subsidy schemes are Member-State-set and unstable. Germany's BEG/GEG turmoil alone drove a ~48% national sales crash in 2024 despite a supportive EU framework — EU-level direction does not guarantee channel demand.
  • two KEY items are not yet LAW: the Ecodesign 2029 fossil-boiler market ban is proposal-stage (consultation late 2025), and EPBD's 2040 fossil-boiler phase-out depends on national transposition (deadline 29 May 2026) and 'credible' national plans — both directionally clear but legally soft today.
  • ETS2 PRICE & timing RISK: start could slip to 2028 if energy prices spike; the realised allowance price (and thus the gas-cost penalty) is uncertain and politically exposed to cost-of-living backlash, which could blunt the main tailwind.
  • F-GAS direction IS genuinely MIXED and under review: the regulation's review clause may release extra HP quota if REPowerEU targets are threatened — the net supply-cost impact on HFC-based lines could move either way through 2025–2029.
  • compliance-COST figures are weak: per-unit cost of the R290 redesign and of Ecodesign re-testing could not be sourced to a primary quantified figure — treated qualitatively, not as confirmed numbers.
  • FX and steel exposures are HYPOTHESIS-tier — no HP-specific quantified sensitivity was located; included for completeness, not as evidenced findings.
  • Unit-count base figures (≈2.1–2.3m per year 2024, −21/−22%) come predominantly from EHPA (single industry body); directionally corroborated by trade press but share a common upstream source — treat the exact percentage as RESEARCHED, not independently triangulated.

Methodology & how to read this

How this study is built, and what the confidence labels mean.

Confidence

CONFIRMED — ≥2 independent sources agree (or independently verified)
RESEARCHED — backed by one cited source
HYPOTHESIS — the model's estimate — unverified, given as a range
CONFLICTING — sources disagree beyond tolerance — shown as a range
SINGLE_SOURCE — only one source found

Audit verdicts

HOLDS — independently corroborated
OVERSTATED — right direction, magnitude too strong
REFUTED — contradicted by what was found
UNCERTAIN — could not be independently confirmed

Source quality

Authoritative — official statistics, regulators, standards bodies, research institutes
Reputable — established press and major industry research
Other — vendor, trade or unattributed — read with care

The pipeline

The market is researched as a fixed sequence of specialist stages, each building on what the earlier ones proved. Every figure is sourced or flagged: a number that cannot be traced is never stated.

1
Value Chain. the routes to market (channels) and the price build-up from end-user price back to ex-works — the one margin.
2
Demand. the end-user demand equation, the growth drivers vs barriers, and what would have to be true for each.
3
Market Size. ≥2 independent sizing pathways, reconciled on one declared basis — the answer is a range, not a point.
4
Participants. who plays at each value-chain stage, their share of the sized market, and concentration.
5
Regulatory & Macro. the named regulations (tailwind/headwind), their timelines, and macro sensitivities.
6
Verification. an adversarial audit that tries to refute the decision-critical claims.
7
Synthesis. the executive read — written only from the verified claims above.

Key terms

Ex-works (factory-gate)
The manufacturer's selling price at its own premises — before transport, distributor and retailer margins, and VAT. The smallest, 'truest' measure of a market: manufacturer revenue.
End-user / installed spend
What the final buyer actually pays — ex-works plus every downstream margin, plus (for services) installation/labour and VAT. Often 2–10× the ex-works figure.
Consumer↔manufacturer ratio
The multiple between end-user price and ex-works price for a channel — used to move a market size between the two bases without distorting it.
Channel
A distinct route a product takes to its buyer (e.g. OEM first-fit vs aftermarket). Price builds up differently per channel, so they are never blended.
Triangulation
Sizing a market by ≥2 independent methods and reconciling them; agreement raises confidence, divergence is itself a finding.
TAM / SAM / SOM
Total / Serviceable / Obtainable market — the whole market, the slice a player can address, and the slice it can realistically win.
Concentration (CR4 / HHI)
How consolidated a market is — CR4 = combined share of the top 4 players; HHI = sum of squared shares.
