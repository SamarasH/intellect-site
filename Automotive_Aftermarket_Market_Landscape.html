Automotive Aftermarket Market Landscape

Europe · June 2026 · benchmark: Autodoc

Autodoc is the structural winner of the European online aftermarket: at €1.55bn revenue (+18.9% in 2024) across 27 countries it is roughly 6–8x the next pure-play, and its moat is pan-European reach against rivals that are national (kfzteile24 ~94% Germany; Oscaro France-led) or captive (Mister-Auto/Stellantis, ATU/Mobivia). The tailwind is channel migration — BCG sees online penetration rising from ~30% to ~70% by 2035 in a €202bn market — not category growth. The two real risks are margin (Amazon/eBay undercut on commodity SKUs — e.g. an oil filter at €6.71 vs €9.52) and, longer-term, EV erosion of the highest-volume categories: filters, ignition and exhaust are HIGH-exposure annuities that shrink as the BEV parc (15.4% of new sales) ages in, while braking is MEDIUM (regen) and suspension/body/tyres are largely insulated.

Market split by part category · EV exposure

Braking (pads, discs, calipers) EV medium
~12–18% (directional est., not cleanly sized; see warnings)
Brake pads ≈29% of the braking-component market (MarketResearchFuture). EV exposure MEDIUM: regenerative braking materially extends pad/rotor life, so EVs still carry brakes but replace them far less often. Brake fluid/lines unaffected. · growth: Stable/up near-term (aging parc); softening long-term as EV parc grows
Filters (oil, air, fuel, cabin) EV high
~8–12% (directional est.; aftermarket = >60% of filter volume)
Aftermarket is >60% of filter sales volume; oil filters the largest sub-segment (GMInsights). EV exposure HIGH: BEVs need no engine oil, oil filter, fuel filter or air filter — only the cabin/pollen filter survives. A core ICE-only annuity. · growth: Stable near-term; structural decline long-term
Service / wear (spark plugs, belts, oils, exhaust) EV high
~10–15% (directional est.; not separately sized)
EV exposure HIGH/HIGH-RISK: BEVs have no spark plugs, no exhaust/catalytic converter, no engine oil, single-speed transmission (~20 powertrain moving parts vs hundreds). Exhaust and ignition are the most EV-exposed categories of all. · growth: Stable near-term; high structural decline long-term
Suspension & steering (shocks, arms, bushings) EV low
~8–12% (directional est.; not separately sized)
EV exposure LOW: suspension is drivetrain-agnostic and EV battery weight increases load on shocks/bushings, if anything supporting demand. A relative safe-haven category. · growth: Stable/up (heavier EVs stress chassis)
Electrical / ignition / batteries (12V) EV medium
~8–12% (directional est.; not separately sized)
EV exposure MEDIUM: ignition components disappear, but EVs still use a 12V auxiliary battery and far more electronics/sensors. Net content per vehicle is rising even as ignition-specific parts vanish. · growth: Up (electronics content rising)
Body / lighting EV low
~10–15% (directional est.; 'body part' = 2nd-largest segment 2024)
Body parts were the second-largest aftermarket segment in 2024 (Fortune). EV exposure LOW: collision, body and lighting parts are powertrain-independent. · growth: Stable/up
Tyres & wheels EV low
~28% of replacement-parts value (2024)
Largest single replacement category at 28.3% (2024). EV exposure LOW (even positive): EVs' weight + instant torque wear tyres FASTER, raising replacement frequency. Note: tyres are largely outside the core SKU mix of the online pure-plays in this brief. · growth: Up

How this is built

A verified-only competitive scan of the European online car-parts aftermarket: market sizing, a split by part category with EV-exposure, a competitor landscape, and a price matrix. A price appears only if verified against a real listing — retailer pages bot-block scraping, so prices are sourced from web-search snippets; a cell is left blank (—) rather than guessed.

Key terms

Ex-works (factory-gate)
The manufacturer's selling price at its own premises — before transport, distributor and retailer margins, and VAT. The smallest, 'truest' measure of a market: manufacturer revenue.
End-user / installed spend
What the final buyer actually pays — ex-works plus every downstream margin, plus (for services) installation/labour and VAT. Often 2–10× the ex-works figure.
Consumer↔manufacturer ratio
The multiple between end-user price and ex-works price for a channel — used to move a market size between the two bases without distorting it.
Channel
A distinct route a product takes to its buyer (e.g. OEM first-fit vs aftermarket). Price builds up differently per channel, so they are never blended.
Triangulation
Sizing a market by ≥2 independent methods and reconciling them; agreement raises confidence, divergence is itself a finding.
TAM / SAM / SOM
Total / Serviceable / Obtainable market — the whole market, the slice a player can address, and the slice it can realistically win.
Concentration (CR4 / HHI)
How consolidated a market is — CR4 = combined share of the top 4 players; HHI = sum of squared shares.
