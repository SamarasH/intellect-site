Why the prices differ

Brand-driven spread (tier premium)

  • Three clear tiers on the same part: BUDGET private-label (Stark €7.29, Ridex €7.28–7.59) → PREMIUM aftermarket (Hengst €9.99, Mann/Mahle/Bosch €9.78–10.99, Filtron/Purflux €11.99) → GENUINE VW (€17.69 on eBay).
  • Premium IAM commands roughly +35–65% over budget; genuine VW roughly +140% over the cheapest budget cell — despite the genuine filter being physically built by one of the premium OE suppliers (Mann/Mahle).
  • Premium aftermarket clusters tightly (€9.78–11.99, ~22% band): Mann/Mahle/Bosch are near-identical because all are OE-grade and price-transparent. Febi at €7.99 straddles budget and premium.
  • Implication: the genuine-vs-aftermarket premium is paying for the VW box, not measurably different filtration — the premium IAM tier captures most of the quality at ~40% lower cost.

Platform-driven spread (channel margin)

  • Specialist price-led retailers (Autodoc group) set the market floor via aggressive RRP discounting (−47% to −56% vs UVP shown on-page) and their own budget labels — the cheapest verified cells all sit here.
  • Marketplaces sit higher: the only marketplace price captured, genuine VW on eBay at €17.69, is well above the specialist aftermarket band; Amazon/eBay pricing is set by third-party resellers and skews toward genuine/branded SKUs.
  • Aggregators (idealo/daparto/Geizhals) compress prices further — Geizhals lists Mann W712/95 from €5.90, below any specialist cell — by exposing the long tail of smaller shops on one page.
  • Sister-site effect: autodoc.de and pkwteile.de share supply yet price the same Mann part differently (€10.99 vs €9.78) — proof prices are set per storefront/promo, not by cost.
  • Implication: for a commodity filter the platform choice moves price as much as the brand — an aggregator-sourced premium filter can undercut a budget filter bought on a marketplace.

Observed spread (cheapest vs dearest)

  • Within the requested platform set, verified cells run from Stark €7.29 (autodoc.de) to genuine VW €17.69 (eBay.de) — a spread of €10.40, or +143%.
  • Restricting to like-for-like premium aftermarket, the band is tight: €9.78 (Mann, pkwteile) to €11.99 (Filtron/Purflux, autodoc) = +23%.
  • Including the comparable aggregator Geizhals (Mann W712/95 from €5.90, outside the requested set), the effective floor drops ~19% below the cheapest in-set cell — showing aggregators set the true market low.
  • Implication: a DIY buyer can pay anywhere from ~€6 to ~€18 for a functionally equivalent oil filter; tier + channel, not the part, explain the entire ~3× range.
