Technical notes (7)
  • Headline is a RANGE (EUR 1.6-3.0bn manufacturer price, most-likely EUR 2.0-2.5bn), not a point. A single number here would be false precision.
  • BASIS DISCIPLINE: all figures are manufacturer price. Equivalent END-USER spend is ~3x higher (~EUR 6-9bn) because of stacked channel margins (OES 3.08x). Do not mix the two.
  • Load-bearing assumption is pad life per axle (HYPOTHESIS). Halving the assumed life would roughly double Pathway A. Verify with real fitment/wear telematics before relying on the upper end.
  • Syndicated global brake-pad reports (Mordor, Fortune, etc.) disagree with each other 2x+ on absolute size and fail a unit reality check; treat their absolute EU levels as unreliable. Use them only for growth/share direction.
  • Channel unit-split (IAM 60/OES 25/DIY 15) is inferred from VALUE shares, not measured unit shares; unit DIY share is likely understated vs value share given lower DIY prices.
  • GAP / recommended next step: ground apparent-consumption directly from Eurostat PRODCOM (29.32.30 mounted brake linings) + COMEXT HS 8708.30.10 trade. IndexBox indicates EU brake-component exports ~EUR 0.6-0.8bn and Chinese/Indian/Turkish pad imports ~EUR 0.4-0.55bn/yr, but those scopes are broader than PC pads and were not isolated here.
  • EV regen is currently a minor drag (~0.97 factor at 3.7% BEV/PHEV parc) but is the structural inflection variable; watch in-service-parc electrification x per-car mileage, not new-registration BEV share.

↓ Barriers (5)

#Factor / applicabilityMarket impactEvidence status
1
EV regenerative braking collapses pads-per-car-year (the load-bearing suppressor)
Replacement (OES+IAM+DIY) · structural
Mechanism and evidence
Effect: down — acts PER UNIT even as the parc grows. regen cuts friction-brake wear 64–95%; BEV pads last 2–4× longer → wear_factor 0.25–0.50. BEV share of NEW regs already 13.6% (2024) → 17.4% (2025); as these flow into the parc, replacement units per electrified car structurally fall.
Must hold: For this to STALL/SHRINK the market, BEV (and one-pedal PHEV) penetration of the parc must rise faster than parc-size + ageing add wear — i.e. low-wear vehicles must displace high-wear ICE replacement events quicker than the base grows.
Context and evidence

Today only 3.7% of the parc is plug-in, so the bite is small but compounding.

Structurally falling per-car mileage
Replacement (OES+IAM+DIY) · structural
Mechanism and evidence
Effect: down. km/car fell ~11% 2000→2018 (13,266→11,839); ~10,800 in 2023 — fewer km means slower wear and longer replacement intervals per car
Must hold: For this to suppress demand, the long-run downward mileage trend (modal shift, urbanisation, remote work, fuel cost) must persist and outpace parc growth; a sustained mileage rebound would neutralise it.

Must hold: New replacement pads and braking-assist tech must measurably lengthen the average replacement interval across the parc, lowering events/car/yr even for ICE vehicles.

Cross-channel (OES → IAM → DIY) · structural

Euro 7 (Regulation (EU) 2024/1257) — brake particulate (PM10) emission limitsmixed CONFIRMED src
Adopted 2024. Brake-PM limits apply at type-approval from 29 Nov 2026 for newly type-approved M1 (passenger) vehicles and from 29 Nov 2027 for all new registrations. Single 3 mg/km limit harmonised across all classes from ~2035. Test per UN GTR No. 24 (WLTP brake cycle). Aftermarket extension via ECE R90 still being finalised in committee (no fixed date).
First-ever EU cap on non-exhaust brake-wear particles: 7 mg/km PM10 per vehicle (all four corners) for ICE/HEV/FCEV, tighter 3 mg/km for pure BEV. Forces reformulated low-emission friction material plus, in most cases, hard-coated (e.g. tungsten-carbide/laser-deposited) grey-cast-iron discs. Compliance over full vehicle lifetime (in-service conformity / market surveillance). → MIXED and the single load-bearing instrument. VALUE-ACCRETIVE: ~60% of current series-production brakes exceed the limit (measured 6–20 mg/km), so OE pads must move to premium, copper-reduced, application-tailored, R90-recertified friction material — lifting manufacturer price ASP and re-anchoring the OES/IAM price ladder upward; the requirement filters into the IAM via ECE R90, raising the replacement-pad spec floor. VOLUME-AMBIGUOUS-to-NEGATIVE: the tighter 3 mg/km BEV limit and lifetime-compliance design incentive reward regen-heavy, low-friction-wear systems — reinforcing the per-car wear collapse that is the market's structural suppressor. Net: pushes top-line value up while nudging long-run unit-wear down.
Compliance cost: Material/process redesign at OE: coated-disc retooling (grinding-line conversion) + bespoke friction mixes; supplier examples (Brembo Greentell, Tenneco, TMD) cite ~90% emission cuts. EU per-vehicle Euro-7 cost estimates are modest in the Commission IA but OEMs claim €2,000+ total per car across all Euro 7 items (brakes are a fraction, not isolated). Aftermarket recertification cost per R90 part is a real but unquantified incremental burden.
EU End-of-Life Vehicles Regulation (proposal COM(2023)451; Council–Parliament provisional agreement 12 Dec 2025) — vehicle circularitymixed CONFIRMED src
Commission proposal 13 Jul 2023; provisional political agreement reached 12 Dec 2025; awaits formal adoption. Applies ~2 years after entry into force. Recycled-plastic targets: 15% within 6 yrs, 25% within 10 yrs. Steel/aluminium recycled-content targets via delegated act ~2 yrs after entry; copper/magnesium/CRM targets after a feasibility study (within 1 yr of entry).

Macro sensitivity model (5)
Interest rates → new-vehicle build/registrations (OE line-fit demand)
OE line-fit units are driven by EU27 build rate (≈10.6M, structurally below peak), which is financing-sensitive: higher policy/loan rates suppress new-car demand and depress the cyclical OE recovery off its depressed base, shrinking the one-set-per-axle factory-fitment volume. Aftermarket (wear-driven) is largely insulated from rates — even counter-cyclical, as high rates lengthen vehicle holding periods, age the parc, and shift spend toward independents. Net macro-rate exposure of the total pad market is therefore modest and concentrated in the smallest-value channel (OE at €15/set manufacturer price).
Steel price + CBAM overlay (backing plates, steel fibre)
Semi-metallic friction material is ~30–70% metal (steel, iron, copper) and every pad has a steel backing plate; steel is the single largest commodity in the bill of materials. Steel price moves and the new CBAM carbon cost on imported steel feed directly into manufacturer price pad cost. A material exposure on the cost base, partly hedgeable via EU/recycled steel sourcing.
Copper price (semi-metallic/ceramic friction material)
Copper/brass fibres are a meaningful constituent of semi-metallic and many ceramic pads (heat transfer, friction), so LME copper (USD-priced) is a direct input-cost driver. Exposure is structurally declining as Euro 7-era reformulation and global copper-free mandates engineer copper out — turning a present cost risk into a diminishing one over the decade.
Energy prices (mixing, pressing, resin curing/sintering)
Pad manufacture is process-energy-intensive (hot-pressing, thermal curing of phenolic-resin binders, post-bake); EU electricity/gas price levels sit over the conversion-cost line. Euro 7 coated-disc grinding/coating lines add further energy intensity at the OE system level. A persistent EU competitiveness exposure versus lower-energy-cost Asian friction-material imports.
EUR/USD FX on USD-priced inputs and Asian imports
Key inputs are USD-linked: LME-priced copper, graphite, and a large share of imported friction-material/raw inputs and finished low-cost pads sourced from Asia. A weaker EUR raises landed input and finished-import cost (squeezing EU converter margin but improving relative competitiveness of EU-made pads); a stronger EUR does the reverse. Second-order versus commodity and regulatory drivers.

Full regulatory interpretation

The regulatory tilt is net POSITIVE on VALUE and net NEUTRAL-to-NEGATIVE on long-run VOLUME, and the single most material instrument is Euro 7 (Reg (EU) 2024/1257) brake-particulate limits — the first-ever EU cap on brake-wear PM (7 mg/km ICE, 3 mg/km BEV), biting at type-approval from 29 Nov 2026 (new types) and 29 Nov 2027 (all new registrations), then filtering into the independent aftermarket via ECE R90 across 2027–2030. Because ~60% of today's brakes fail the limit, Euro 7 forces a wholesale move to premium, copper-reduced, coated-disc-matched friction systems — re-anchoring manufacturer price ASP upward and lifting the value of every channel's set, the clearest near-term tailwind. Working the same direction on the demand side, MVBER (to May 2028) and the Data Act (live since Sep 2025) lock in independent-channel data/parts access, defending IAM/DIY capture of replacement-pad units. The offsets are (a) Euro 7's own design logic — the tighter BEV limit and lifetime-compliance rules reward regen-heavy low-wear braking, reinforcing the electrification wear-collapse that is the market's structural volume suppressor; and (b) mild cost headwinds from CBAM on imported steel (definitive period since 1 Jan 2026, €75.36/tCO2 Q1 2026) and ELV circularity obligations (provisional deal 12 Dec 2025).   • Euro 7 compliance-cost figures are not cleanly isolable for brakes: the '€2,000+ per car' OEM claim covers ALL Euro 7 items, not brake friction specifically; treat per-pad cost deltas as directionally up but unquantified.

The pipeline

The market is researched as a fixed sequence of specialist stages, each building on prior working research. Verification then determines which scoped claims can enter the narrative; unresolved estimates stay explicitly unestablished.

1
Lead Plan. the approved boundary, declared evidence needs and confirmation criteria.
2
Data Pack. the mapped deterministic APIs and source-traced series retrieved before agent analysis.
3
Value Chain. the routes to market (channels) and the price build-up from end-user price back to manufacturer price, where comparable observations support the steps.
4
Regulatory & Macro. enacted rules, proposals and timing before policy-dependent demand and scenarios.
5
Demand. the end-user demand equation, the growth drivers vs barriers, and what would have to be true for each.
6
Market Size. aim for at least two independent sizing pathways on one declared basis; show a reconciled range only when the evidence supports it.
7
Industry Structure & Value Pools. submarkets, component economics and category shares reconciled against the defined market denominator.
8
Participants. who plays at each value-chain stage, their share of the sized market, and concentration.
9
Verification. an adversarial audit that tries to refute the decision-critical claims.
10
Evidence Repair. one bounded attempt to narrow, support or retract failed material claims, followed by re-verification.
11
Expert Panel. named people, roles, affiliations and public statements checked separately. Study-specific coverage thresholds are shown in Expert Perspectives; identity counts do not validate claims.
12
Quotation Selection. on-point supporting and challenging voices selected only from the verified panel, followed by exact-source reopening.
13
Synthesis. the executive read — written only from eligible claim revisions; panel opinion remains interpretive context.
14
Final Story Review. a bounded review of the composed report against its evidence; publishing requires current approval and artifact integrity.

Key terms

Manufacturer price
The manufacturer's selling price at its own premises — before transport, downstream margins and VAT. Use only when the approved market boundary is manufacturer revenue.
End-user / installed spend
Spending at the final buyer boundary. Hardware, installation, civil works, grid works and tax treatment must match the declared study scope; there is no universal conversion multiple.
Consumer↔manufacturer ratio
The multiple between end-user price and manufacturer price for a channel — used to move a market size between the two bases without distorting it.
Channel
A distinct route a product takes to its buyer (e.g. OEM first-fit vs aftermarket). Price builds up differently per channel, so they are never blended.
Triangulation
Sizing a market by ≥2 independent methods and reconciling them; agreement raises confidence, divergence is itself a finding.
TAM / SAM / SOM
Total / Serviceable / Obtainable market — the whole market, the slice a player can address, and the slice it can realistically win.
Concentration (CR4 / HHI)
How consolidated a market is — CR4 = combined share of the top 4 players; HHI = sum of squared shares.
