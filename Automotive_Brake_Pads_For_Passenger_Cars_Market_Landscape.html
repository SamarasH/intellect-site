Value Chain — Brake pads for passenger cars

EU27 · one chain, 4 route(s) to market

Brake pads split first into OE line-fit (B2B, sold to carmakers near ex-works with no distribution layer) vs replacement aftermarket; the aftermarket then splits into the captive OES dealer route, the independent-workshop (IAM) route, and direct-to-consumer online. The split matters because each route stacks a different number of intermediary margins, so end-user spend overstates manufacturer revenue by a channel-specific multiple — blending them would mis-size the market.

Net thesis — the contested condition

Units are set to be roughly flat-to-modestly-positive in the near term — a growing (256M+, +1.4%/yr) and ageing (12.7yr) parc keeps lifting replacement events, while OE line-fit recovers only cyclically off a structurally depressed ~10.6M build. But the trajectory does NOT net to one number, because drivers and barriers point opposite ways on different channels: OE is a build-rate story (cyclical up, structural down vs peak), the aftermarket is a wear story, and within the aftermarket the mix is migrating from OES toward IAM/DIY — lifting independent/online UNITS while compressing top-line VALUE per set (€130→€80→€45). The single contested condition the whole market hinges on is the EV regenerative-braking per-unit wear collapse: regen cuts friction wear 64–95% and 2–4×-longer pad life, but BEV/PHEV are still only ~3.7% of the parc. The market grows for as long as parc-size + ageing add wear faster than electrification removes it; it inflects to decline the year BEV penetration of the in-service parc (not of new registrations) crosses the point where displaced high-wear ICE replacement events outrun the growing base. Watch parc-electrification rate × per-car mileage — not new-car BEV share — as the leading indicator.

Regulatory & Macro — brake pads for passenger cars

EU27 · what bounds the trajectory

Euro 7 (Regulation (EU) 2024/1257) — brake particulate (PM10) emission limitsmixed CONFIRMED src
Adopted 2024. Brake-PM limits apply at type-approval from 29 Nov 2026 for newly type-approved M1 (passenger) vehicles and from 29 Nov 2027 for all new registrations. Single 3 mg/km limit harmonised across all classes from ~2035. Test per UN GTR No. 24 (WLTP brake cycle). Aftermarket extension via ECE R90 still being finalised in committee (no fixed date).
First-ever EU cap on non-exhaust brake-wear particles: 7 mg/km PM10 per vehicle (all four corners) for ICE/HEV/FCEV, tighter 3 mg/km for pure BEV. Forces reformulated low-emission friction material plus, in most cases, hard-coated (e.g. tungsten-carbide/laser-deposited) grey-cast-iron discs. Compliance over full vehicle lifetime (in-service conformity / market surveillance). → MIXED and the single load-bearing instrument. VALUE-ACCRETIVE: ~60% of current series-production brakes exceed the limit (measured 6–20 mg/km), so OE pads must move to premium, copper-reduced, application-tailored, R90-recertified friction material — lifting ex-works ASP and re-anchoring the OES/IAM price ladder upward; the requirement filters into the IAM via ECE R90, raising the replacement-pad spec floor. VOLUME-AMBIGUOUS-to-NEGATIVE: the tighter 3 mg/km BEV limit and lifetime-compliance design incentive reward regen-heavy, low-friction-wear systems — reinforcing the per-car wear collapse that is the market's structural suppressor. Net: pushes top-line value up while nudging long-run unit-wear down.
Compliance cost: Material/process redesign at OE: coated-disc retooling (grinding-line conversion) + bespoke friction mixes; supplier examples (Brembo Greentell, Tenneco, TMD) cite ~90% emission cuts. EU per-vehicle Euro-7 cost estimates are modest in the Commission IA but OEMs claim €2,000+ total per car across all Euro 7 items (brakes are a fraction, not isolated). Aftermarket recertification cost per R90 part is a real but unquantified incremental burden.
EU End-of-Life Vehicles Regulation (proposal COM(2023)451; Council–Parliament provisional agreement 12 Dec 2025) — vehicle circularitymixed CONFIRMED src
Commission proposal 13 Jul 2023; provisional political agreement reached 12 Dec 2025; awaits formal adoption. Applies ~2 years after entry into force. Recycled-plastic targets: 15% within 6 yrs, 25% within 10 yrs. Steel/aluminium recycled-content targets via delegated act ~2 yrs after entry; copper/magnesium/CRM targets after a feasibility study (within 1 yr of entry).
Circular-design rules: reusability/recyclability/recoverability thresholds, recycled-content mandates (plastics now; steel, aluminium, copper, CRMs to follow), and parts/materials information & labelling. Estimated to recover ~5–6 Mt steel and 0.2–0.3 Mt copper annually across the fleet. → MIXED, second-order on pads specifically. Raises design-compliance and material-declaration burden across the vehicle, and recycled-content mandates could lift near-term input cost. Longer-term the same secondary-material recovery (steel backing plates, copper) could ease and de-risk pad input sourcing. Marginal direct effect on brake-pad units/value vs Euro 7.
Compliance cost: Material declaration/labelling and recycled-content sourcing premiums; magnitude for a brake-pad maker is low relative to OEM-level circularity obligations, and unquantified for friction parts.

Adopted 17 Apr 2023; prolongs MVBER to 31 May 2028; updated Supplementary Guidelines effective 2023. Commission to review again ahead of 2028 expiry.

  • Interest rates → new-vehicle build/registrations (OE line-fit demand): OE line-fit units are driven by EU27 build rate (≈10.6M, structurally below peak), which is financing-sensitive: higher policy/loan rates suppress new-car demand and depress the cyclical OE recovery off its depressed base, shrinking the one-set-per-axle factory-fitment volume. Aftermarket (wear-driven) is largely insulated from rates — even counter-cyclical, as high rates lengthen vehicle holding periods, age the parc, and shift spend toward independents. Net macro-rate exposure of the total pad market is therefore modest and concentrated in the smallest-value channel (OE at €15/set ex-works). HYPOTHESIS
  • Steel price + CBAM overlay (backing plates, steel fibre): Semi-metallic friction material is ~30–70% metal (steel, iron, copper) and every pad has a steel backing plate; steel is the single largest commodity in the bill of materials. Steel price moves and the new CBAM carbon cost on imported steel feed directly into ex-works pad cost. A material exposure on the cost base, partly hedgeable via EU/recycled steel sourcing. The regulatory tilt is net POSITIVE on VALUE and net NEUTRAL-to-NEGATIVE on long-run VOLUME, and the single most material instrument is Euro 7 (Reg (EU) 2024/1257) brake-particulate limits — the first-ever EU cap on brake-wear PM (7 mg/km ICE, 3 mg/km BEV), biting at type-approval from 29 Nov 2026 (new types) and 29 Nov 2027 (all new registrations), then filtering into the independent aftermarket via ECE R90 across 2027–2030. Because ~60% of today's brakes fail the limit, Euro 7 forces a wholesale move to premium, copper-reduced, coated-disc-matched friction systems — re-anchoring ex-works ASP upward and lifting the value of every channel's set, the clearest near-term tailwind. Working the same direction on the demand side, MVBER (to May 2028) and the Data Act (live since Sep 2025) lock in independent-channel data/parts access, defending IAM/DIY capture of replacement-pad units. The offsets are (a) Euro 7's own design logic — the tighter BEV limit and lifetime-compliance rules reward regen-heavy low-wear braking, reinforcing the electrification wear-collapse that is the market's structural volume suppressor; and (b) mild cost headwinds from CBAM on imported steel (definitive period since 1 Jan 2026, €75.36/tCO2 Q1 2026) and ELV circularity obligations (provisional deal 12 Dec 2025). Net: regulation lifts per-set value and protects independent volume in 2026–2030, while quietly accelerating the per-car wear erosion that bounds the market's terminal trajectory.

Methodology & how to read this

How this study is built, and what the confidence labels mean.

Confidence

CONFIRMED — ≥2 independent sources agree (or independently verified)
RESEARCHED — backed by one cited source
HYPOTHESIS — the model's estimate — unverified, given as a range
CONFLICTING — sources disagree beyond tolerance — shown as a range
SINGLE_SOURCE — only one source found

Audit verdicts

HOLDS — independently corroborated
OVERSTATED — right direction, magnitude too strong
REFUTED — contradicted by what was found
UNCERTAIN — could not be independently confirmed

The pipeline

The market is researched as a fixed sequence of specialist stages, each building on what the earlier ones proved. Every figure is sourced or flagged — a number that can't be traced is never stated.

1
Value Chain. the routes to market (channels) and the price build-up from end-user price back to ex-works — the one margin.
2
Demand. the end-user demand equation, the growth drivers vs barriers, and what would have to be true for each.
3
Market Size. ≥2 independent sizing pathways, reconciled on one declared basis — the answer is a range, not a point.
4
Participants. who plays at each value-chain stage, their share of the sized market, and concentration.
5
Regulatory & Macro. the named regulations (tailwind/headwind), their timelines, and macro sensitivities.
6
Verification. an adversarial audit that tries to refute the decision-critical claims.
7
Synthesis. the executive read — written only from the verified claims above.

Key terms

Ex-works (factory-gate)
The manufacturer's selling price at its own premises — before transport, distributor and retailer margins, and VAT. The smallest, 'truest' measure of a market: manufacturer revenue.
End-user / installed spend
What the final buyer actually pays — ex-works plus every downstream margin, plus (for services) installation/labour and VAT. Often 2–10× the ex-works figure.
Consumer↔manufacturer ratio
The multiple between end-user price and ex-works price for a channel — used to move a market size between the two bases without distorting it.
Channel
A distinct route a product takes to its buyer (e.g. OEM first-fit vs aftermarket). Price builds up differently per channel, so they are never blended.
Triangulation
Sizing a market by ≥2 independent methods and reconciling them; agreement raises confidence, divergence is itself a finding.
TAM / SAM / SOM
Total / Serviceable / Obtainable market — the whole market, the slice a player can address, and the slice it can realistically win.
Concentration (CR4 / HHI)
How consolidated a market is — CR4 = combined share of the top 4 players; HHI = sum of squared shares.
