Executive Summary

Executive Summary

What should the reader know and do?

EU27 · written from verified claims only

Don't buy 'critical battery materials' as one basket: EU27 lithium volume grows structurally toward the 2035 phase-out while the LFP shift (~63% of cells, runs hot vs IEA ~55%) hollows out nickel/cobalt per-unit demand even as the fleet grows.
Lithium and nickel/cobalt move in opposite directions and must be sized and underwritten separately, not averaged. The FY2025 market is ~€1,500–3,300M ex-works (CONFIRMED basis but self-built denominator, band spans 2.2×) and is today overwhelmingly Ni/Co chemistry — the part whose intensity is structurally shrinking. The structural and regulatory thesis is decision-grade; the headline size and all CR4/share figures are directional HYPOTHESIS only.

What to do — by stakeholder

Investor (PE deal team): Underwrite a lithium-refining thesis (structurally growing) separately from Ni/Co (structurally decoupling); do not pay basket-blended multiples on a Ni/Co-heavy 2025 revenue base.
Investor (diligence): Treat CR4 ~50–60% / HHI 1,200–1,800 and every company share as directional HYPOTHESIS on a self-built denominator — demand vendor data before relying on any share.

Demand outlook

  • BEV +29.9% in 2025 to 1.88m units, 17.4% share — master lithium lever (CONFIRMED, audit HOLDS verbatim).

Implication: bet on lithium volume growth and against Ni/Co per-unit intensity — and do not attribute near-term recycled tonnes to the 2031 mandate.

Implication: underwrite to the post-2031 mandate timeline and the unresolved 2035 vote — not to an assumed 2025 feedstock-retention windfall.

Value-chain economics

  • Consumer↔manufacturer ratios are thin B2B layers: Direct 1.04×, Merchant 1.11×, Recycled 1.09× (Merchant gap = trader margin) (RESEARCHED).
  • Tax wedge assumed ~0% (intra-EU VAT recoverable, ~0% duty); extra-EU may carry up to ~5.5% MFN — unconfirmed (HYPOTHESIS).

  • Large ore→chemical value-add sits inside the manufacturer; mine-basis sizing needs ~3.8–4.3× adjustment (RESEARCHED).
Implication: route volume through Direct/offtake to capture the ~1.11× Merchant trader margin, and re-price the anchor at the moment of sizing — never carry a static $18k/t.

Net thesis — the contested condition

The basket label 'critical battery materials' masks two opposite trajectories that must not be averaged. LITHIUM: the volume top line grows structurally and across both virgin channels — driven by the 1.88 m per year (+30%) BEV ramp toward the 2035 phase-out plus a 10×-by-2030 BESS buildout — and survives the per-kWh thrifting headwind. NICKEL & COBALT: demand is structurally decoupling from the EV ramp because the LFP shift (≈63% of cells) is cutting per-unit intensity faster (Co −2/3, Ni −1/3 since 2020) than units are added — so on the Direct and Merchant channels these materials can stall or shrink in tonnes EVEN AS the EV fleet grows. By channel: Direct/offtake expands its share (bankability/floors); Merchant is the cyclical residual amplified by restocking; the Recycled loop is mandate-pulled but feedstock-CAPPED until ~2030, making it supply-constrained, not demand-constrained, in the near term. The single contested condition the whole trajectory hinges on: whether EU BEV adoption holds its trajectory toward the 2035 ICE phase-out — that is the master lever for virgin lithium volume; and, conditionally on it holding, whether continued LFP penetration permanently hollows out the EU cobalt/nickel thesis even while lithium climbs.

Flagged assumptions & gaps

  • Do not average drivers and barriers across the basket: lithium demand points UP while cobalt/nickel per-unit demand points DOWN on the same (Direct/Merchant) channels. Reporting a single 'critical battery materials' demand trend would be a category error.
  • Material intensity (kg/kWh) varies widely by chemistry and source, and LiOH-monohydrate vs LCE conversion adds error — intensity terms are RESEARCHED ranges, not point estimates.
  • The Direct-vs-Merchant volume split is not publicly disaggregated for EU27; the offtake-coverage ratio (~60–75%) is a HYPOTHESIS inferred from project-finance norms, not a measured share.
  • Recycled-content mandates under Reg 2023/1542 are not binding until August 2031; near-term recycled-channel demand is voluntary/feedstock-driven, so attributing today's recycled volume to the mandate would overstate the pull.
  • BESS material tonnage was approximated using EV lithium intensity as a proxy; stationary storage is increasingly LFP/sodium with lower lithium and ~zero Ni/Co intensity, which would further widen the lithium-vs-Ni/Co divergence.
  • This is a demand-side read only: it does not size the market or weigh supply/competitor capacity. Several barriers (price, FIDs, feedstock) are supply-linked and flagged only for their demand-routing effect.

The same battery-grade materials reach cell/cathode makers via three distinct routes that must be sized separately: direct long-term offtake (no intermediary, favorable fixed/indexed terms), the merchant/spot market (carries a trader gross margin and exchange/CIF pricing), and the recycled loop (black-mass→hydromet, driven by EU recycled-content mandates and a different feedstock cost base).

It is the most decision-critical claim yet the least independently verifiable: no third party sizes this exact denominator, the band spans 2.2×, and — most importantly — the workstream's own flagged denominator–NUMERATOR mismatch is real: the market was sized via a lithium-hydroxide proxy whose upstream anchor ($18k/t) I independently confirmed is ~60–80% too high for 2025 spot (~$10k), while actual FY2025 revenue is overwhelmingly Ni/Co chemistry whose intensity is structurally falling (LFP shift, independently confirmed). So the headline is anchored to the one material whose price was overstated and whose volume share of the basket is shrinking.
Overall reliability. RELIABLE on the load-bearing structural and regulatory claims; SOFT on the quantitative headline. Eight of ten checks corroborated independently — the four regulatory instruments (Batteries Reg recycled-content, CRMA benchmarks, 2035 CO2 softening, black-mass hazardous classification), the two demand levers (BEV +30% to 1.88m, BESS 27.1 GWh/+45%/750 GWh-by-2030), the LFP→Ni/Co decoupling thesis, and Terrafame's asset facts all hold, several verbatim. The weaknesses are confined to magnitudes, not direction: the value-chain $18k/t lithium anchor is overstated (the team caught it), the LFP cell-share (~63%) runs ~8pts hot vs IEA, the black-mass ban timing is pulled ~20 months early, and all company shares/the headline size are the team's own estimates on a self-built denominator with no external corroboration. Crucially, the findings are admirably self-aware — nearly every soft spot is already GAP-flagged and labeled HYPOTHESIS, which is the right posture. Treat the structural thesis as decision-grade; treat the €1.5–3.3B headline and all CR4/share figures as directional only.

Methodology & how to read this

How this study is built, and what the confidence labels mean.

Confidence
CONFIRMED — ≥2 independent sources agree (or independently verified)
RESEARCHED — backed by one cited source
HYPOTHESIS — the model's estimate — unverified, given as a range
CONFLICTING — sources disagree beyond tolerance — shown as a range
SINGLE_SOURCE — only one source found
Audit verdicts
HOLDS — independently corroborated
OVERSTATED — right direction, magnitude too strong
REFUTED — contradicted by what was found
UNCERTAIN — could not be independently confirmed
Source quality
Authoritative — official statistics, regulators, standards bodies, research institutes
Reputable — established press and major industry research
Other — vendor, trade or unattributed — read with care

The pipeline

The market is researched as a fixed sequence of specialist stages, each building on what the earlier ones proved. Every figure is sourced or flagged: a number that cannot be traced is never stated.

1
Value Chain. the routes to market (channels) and the price build-up from end-user price back to ex-works — the one margin.
2
Demand. the end-user demand equation, the growth drivers vs barriers, and what would have to be true for each.
3
Market Size. ≥2 independent sizing pathways, reconciled on one declared basis — the answer is a range, not a point.
4
Participants. who plays at each value-chain stage, their share of the sized market, and concentration.
5
Regulatory & Macro. the named regulations (tailwind/headwind), their timelines, and macro sensitivities.
6
Verification. an adversarial audit that tries to refute the decision-critical claims.
7
Synthesis. the executive read — written only from the verified claims above.

Key terms

Ex-works (factory-gate)
The manufacturer's selling price at its own premises — before transport, distributor and retailer margins, and VAT. The smallest, 'truest' measure of a market: manufacturer revenue.
End-user / installed spend
What the final buyer actually pays — ex-works plus every downstream margin, plus (for services) installation/labour and VAT. Often 2–10× the ex-works figure.
Consumer↔manufacturer ratio
The multiple between end-user price and ex-works price for a channel — used to move a market size between the two bases without distorting it.
Channel
A distinct route a product takes to its buyer (e.g. OEM first-fit vs aftermarket). Price builds up differently per channel, so they are never blended.
Triangulation
Sizing a market by ≥2 independent methods and reconciling them; agreement raises confidence, divergence is itself a finding.
TAM / SAM / SOM
Total / Serviceable / Obtainable market — the whole market, the slice a player can address, and the slice it can realistically win.
Concentration (CR4 / HHI)
How consolidated a market is — CR4 = combined share of the top 4 players; HHI = sum of squared shares.
