Technical notes (8)
  • No 2025 EU27 COMEXT or PRODCOM extraction was available, so apparent consumption could not be built as the preferred independent trade pathway.
  • The 52 GWh CAM figure covers Europe in the source, not an explicit EU27 country aggregation; non-EU leakage is unresolved.
  • Published figures describe capacity, not verified 2025 output. The 45-65% utilisation and 90-95% yield ranges are HYPOTHESIS assumptions.
  • Nickel sulphate and cobalt sulphate lack transparent EU27 annual-average manufacturer price prices. Price ranges bridge global or Chinese assessments to Europe and therefore remain HYPOTHESIS.
  • Direct and trader/importer channels cannot be separated because no 2025 procurement-share evidence was found. Both are valued together at the same manufacturer price basis.
  • Captive transfers, toll conversion, inventory changes and non-battery leakage cannot be removed from trade without transaction-level or mapped code data.
  • The stated SAM and SOM are investor scenarios only. No evidence supports a particular platform's qualification coverage, capacity, customer access or achievable share.
  • The supplied lithium-carbonate waterfall was illustrative and was not applied to nickel or cobalt; all pathways were built directly on manufacturer price price assumptions to avoid a false cross-material conversion.

Regulation (EU) 2023/1542 of 12 July 2023 concerning batteries and waste batteries, Article 8 recycled-content requirementstailwind CONFIRMED
Recycled-content disclosure applies from 18 August 2028 or 24 months after the calculation-method delegated act enters into force, whichever is later. Minimum recycled content applies from 18 August 2031: cobalt 16%, lithium 6% and nickel 6%. From 18 August 2036 the minima rise to cobalt 26%, lithium 12% and nickel 15%. The Commission must assess possible revisions by 31 December 2028 and may amend the targets by 18 August 2029.
Covered electric-vehicle, SLI and industrial batteries above 2 kWh must document recovered cobalt, lithium and nickel by battery model, year and manufacturing plant, then demonstrate compliance with minimum recovered-content shares. The legal duty falls on battery economic operators, but qualifying recyclers and chemical refiners must provide traceable material data to customers. → Creates a legally protected EU demand pool for battery-grade secondary lithium, nickel and cobalt. It supports recycling and secondary refining but displaces part of virgin-material demand rather than increasing total metal consumption.
Compliance cost: No authoritative EU-wide euro estimate was found. Expected cost categories are chain-of-custody systems, sampling and mass-balance controls, technical documentation, customer audits and verification. Treat any project-level cost estimate as a GAP until the delegated calculation method and facility data are available.
Regulation (EU) 2023/1542 of 12 July 2023 concerning batteries and waste batteries, Articles 7 and 47-53, as amended by Regulation (EU) 2025/1561 of 18 July 2025mixed CONFIRMED
Battery due-diligence policies were postponed from 18 August 2025 to 18 August 2027. Carbon-footprint declarations, performance classes and maximum thresholds phase in by battery category only after the relevant delegated and implementing acts, with the Regulation using 'whichever is later' safeguards. A battery passport is required from 18 February 2027 for EV, LMT and industrial batteries above 2 kWh.
In-scope battery-market operators must identify and manage social and environmental risks in sourcing, processing and trading cobalt, lithium and nickel; obtain third-party verification; retain records for 10 years; and provide prescribed carbon-footprint and passport information. These are downstream legal obligations, but refiners will face contractual demands for origin, processing and emissions data. → Favours traceable, lower-carbon EU refining and recycling, but raises qualification and data costs. The two-year due-diligence delay postpones the commercial premium for compliant supply and demonstrates implementation risk.
Compliance cost: No validated market-wide euro amount is available. Direct cost categories include supply-chain mapping, emissions measurement, IT integration, notified-body verification, periodic audit and ten-year document retention. Costs borne specifically by refiners depend on customer contract allocation and remain a GAP.
Regulation (EU) 2024/1252 of 11 April 2024 establishing a framework for ensuring a secure and sustainable supply of critical raw materials, the Critical Raw Materials Acttailwind CONFIRMED
In force since 23 May 2024. By 2030, EU capacity should approach or reach 10% of annual consumption in extraction, 40% in processing and 25% in recycling, while no more than 65% of annual consumption at a relevant processing stage should come from one third country. Strategic processing or recycling projects have a 15-month permit-granting ceiling, or 12 months for qualifying existing or expansion projects, subject to stated exclusions and extensions.
Member States and the Commission must strengthen strategic-raw-material capacity, monitor supply risks and operate a Strategic Project framework. Project recognition does not waive environmental, permitting or other substantive legal requirements. → Improves the policy and permitting case for EU lithium, battery-grade nickel and cobalt processing and recycling. The benchmarks are system-level policy benchmarks, not guaranteed purchase volumes or automatic subsidies, so they do not by themselves establish project bankability.
Compliance cost: Strategic Project applications require technical evidence, project classification, permit schedules and public-acceptance measures. No standard euro application cost is specified. Faster permitting can reduce carrying cost, but the benefit is project-specific and unquantified.

Full regulatory interpretation

The regulatory tilt is positive for EU27 secondary refining and cautiously positive for local low-carbon processing, but the benefit arrives in stages and is partly offset by permitting, traceability and environmental-compliance costs. The Critical Raw Materials Act supports projects now through strategic status and shorter permitting, while the strongest demand pull begins with recycled-content documentation after 2028 and binding minimum shares from 18 August 2031. Methodology & how to read this

How this study is built, and what the confidence labels mean.

Confidence
CONFIRMED — reported research tier; current independent support is recorded in the source audit
RESEARCHED — backed by one cited source
HYPOTHESIS — a qualitative assumption, scenario or unresolved input; not a verified fact
CONFLICTING — evidence remains unreconciled; no defensible range is implied
SINGLE_SOURCE — one underlying evidence family; multiple URLs are not independent corroboration

The pipeline

The market is researched as a fixed sequence of specialist stages, each building on prior working research. Verification then determines which scoped claims can enter the narrative; unresolved estimates stay explicitly unestablished.

1
Lead Plan. the approved boundary, declared evidence needs and confirmation criteria.
2
Data Pack. the mapped deterministic APIs and source-traced series retrieved before agent analysis.
3
Value Chain. the routes to market (channels) and the price build-up from end-user price back to manufacturer price, where comparable observations support the steps.
4
Regulatory & Macro. enacted rules, proposals and timing before policy-dependent demand and scenarios.
5
Demand. the end-user demand equation, the growth drivers vs barriers, and what would have to be true for each.
6
Market Size. aim for at least two independent sizing pathways on one declared basis; show a reconciled range only when the evidence supports it.
7
Industry Structure & Value Pools. submarkets, component economics and category shares reconciled against the defined market denominator.
8
Participants. who plays at each value-chain stage, their share of the sized market, and concentration.
9
Verification. an adversarial audit that tries to refute the decision-critical claims.
10
Evidence Repair. one bounded attempt to narrow, support or retract failed material claims, followed by re-verification.
11
Expert Panel. named people, roles, affiliations and public statements checked separately. Study-specific coverage thresholds are shown in Expert Perspectives; identity counts do not validate claims.
12
Quotation Selection. on-point supporting and challenging voices selected only from the verified panel, followed by exact-source reopening.
13
Synthesis. the executive read — written only from eligible claim revisions; panel opinion remains interpretive context.
14
Final Story Review. a bounded review of the composed report against its evidence; publishing requires current approval and artifact integrity.

Key terms

Manufacturer price
The manufacturer's selling price at its own premises — before transport, downstream margins and VAT. Use only when the approved market boundary is manufacturer revenue.
End-user / installed spend
Spending at the final buyer boundary. Hardware, installation, civil works, grid works and tax treatment must match the declared study scope; there is no universal conversion multiple.
Consumer↔manufacturer ratio
The multiple between end-user price and manufacturer price for a channel — used to move a market size between the two bases without distorting it.
Channel
A distinct route a product takes to its buyer (e.g. OEM first-fit vs aftermarket). Price builds up differently per channel, so they are never blended.
Triangulation
Sizing a market by ≥2 independent methods and reconciling them; agreement raises confidence, divergence is itself a finding.
TAM / SAM / SOM
Total / Serviceable / Obtainable market — the whole market, the slice a player can address, and the slice it can realistically win.
Concentration (CR4 / HHI)
How consolidated a market is — CR4 = combined share of the top 4 players; HHI = sum of squared shares.
